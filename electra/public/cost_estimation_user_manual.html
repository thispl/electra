1. Introduction — What This Manual Covers

This manual explains, in plain language, how the Cost Estimation module in the Electra ERP system calculates every financial field — from individual line item costs all the way up to the final Total Bidding Price you quote to your client.

Whether you are a project estimator, sales manager, or finance team member, this guide will help you understand:

  • How each cost component is calculated
  • How overheads and profit are distributed across items
  • How the final bidding price is arrived at
  • What the difference is between MEP and non-MEP divisions
🛈 Good to Know All currency amounts in this manual are in QAR (Qatari Riyal). Percentages are always calculated as a portion of the Total Cost unless stated otherwise.

3. Step-by-Step Workflow — From Cost Entry to Final Price

Here is how the system processes your inputs, from start to finish:

  1. You enter line items in a CE SOW — quantities and unit prices for materials, manpower, equipment, etc.
  2. The system calculates each item’s cost amount using the valuation rate (last purchase cost) from the system.
  3. Section sub-totals are computed — each section (Design, Materials, Installation, etc.) gets a total cost and total selling price.
  4. Total Cost is assembled by adding up all section sub-totals.
  5. You enter overhead percentages (Overhead %, Engineering Overhead %, Contingency %) — the system converts these to amounts.
  6. You enter Net Profit as either an amount or a percentage — the system auto-fills the other.
  7. The system distributes overheads and profit proportionally across all line items to compute selling prices.
  8. Gross Profit is calculated from the difference between selling prices and cost amounts.
  9. Total Bidding Price is computed — this is your final quote to the client.
  10. All values roll up from CE SOW to the parent Cost Estimation document for the overall project summary.

4. Understanding Line Items — The Building Blocks

Every individual row you enter in a CE SOW (a material, a worker, a piece of equipment) is a line item. Each line item has these key calculated fields:

Field How It’s Calculated What It Means
Amount
(Cost Amount)		 Quantity × Unit Price The estimated cost for this single line item.
Cost Amount Quantity × Cost Rate Cost based on the system’s last valuation rate (last purchase price) for that item.
Rate with Overheads
(Selling Rate)		 (Amount + Proportional Overhead) ÷ Quantity The selling price per unit, after overheads and profit are added. (See Section 6 for details.)
Amount with Overheads
(Selling Amount)		 Quantity × Rate with Overheads The total selling price for this line item.

Manpower — A Special Case

For manpower rows, the calculation involves workers, hours, and days:

Manpower Calculations Rate = Unit Price × Working Hours × Days
Amount = Total Workers × Rate
Cost Amount = Total Workers × Cost Rate × Working Hours × Days

6. How Selling Price Is Calculated — Overhead Distribution

One of the most important things to understand is how the system distributes overheads and profit across your line items. This ensures each item’s selling price reflects its fair share of the total project overheads.

The Principle

Items that cost more carry more overhead. The system calculates each item’s share of the total cost and distributes overheads and profit proportionally.

How It Works — Step by Step

  1. The system adds up all line item amounts across all sections to get the Total Selling Price Amount (SPA).
  2. For each item, it calculates what percentage that item represents of the total: Item Share % = Item Amount ÷ Total SPA
  3. It calculates the Total Additions to be distributed: Total Overheads + Net Profit Amount
  4. Each item gets its proportionate share: Proportional Overhead = Item Share % × Total Additions
  5. The item’s selling amount is then: Item Amount + Proportional Overhead
  6. And the selling rate per unit is: Selling Amount ÷ Quantity
💡 Why This Matters This proportional distribution means that expensive items (like large material quantities) will naturally carry a larger share of overheads and profit in their selling price — keeping your pricing fair and accurate across all items.

7. Total Cost of the Project

The Total Cost is the foundation for all other calculations. It is simply the sum of all section cost amounts:

Total Cost Formula Total Cost = Design Cost
  + Raw Materials Cost
  + Supply Materials Cost
  + Finishing Work Cost
  + Accessories Cost
  + Installation Cost
  + Manpower Cost
  + Heavy Equipments Cost
  + Subcontract Cost
  + Finished Goods Cost
🛈 Remember Total Cost is the base cost before any overheads, profit, or business promotion are added. All overhead percentages are applied to this figure.

8. Overheads — General, Engineering & Contingency

Overheads are additional costs that are not directly tied to a specific line item but are necessary to run the project. The system supports three types:

Overhead Type How the Amount Is Calculated What It Covers
General Overhead Total Cost × (Overhead % ÷ 100) Administration, facilities, utilities, and other general project costs.
Engineering Overhead Total Cost × (Engineering Overhead % ÷ 100) Engineering-specific overheads like design software, engineering staff, etc.
Contingency Total Cost × (Contingency % ÷ 100) A buffer for unexpected costs or price fluctuations.

Combined Overhead

Total Overheads Total Overheads = General Overhead Amount + Engineering Overhead Amount + Contingency Amount
💡 Flexible Entry You can enter either the percentage or the amount — the system will auto-calculate the other. If you enter an amount, it reverse-calculates the percentage: Overhead % = (Amount ÷ Total Cost) × 100

9. Gross Profit — What It Means & How It’s Computed All Divisions

Gross Profit is the total profit from all your sections, before overheads are deducted. It represents the markup you have applied across all your line items.

How Gross Profit Amount Is Calculated

Gross Profit Amount Gross Profit = Design Profit
  + Raw Materials Profit
  + Materials Profit
  + Finishing Work Profit
  + Accessories Profit
  + Installation Profit
  + Manpower Profit
  + Heavy Equipments Profit
  + Subcontract Profit
  + Finished Goods Profit
  − Discount Tolerance Amount

Where each section’s profit = Section Selling Amount − Section Cost Amount

Gross Profit Percentage

Gross Profit % Gross Profit % = (Gross Profit Amount ÷ Total Bidding Price) × 100

10. Net Profit — Setting Your Profit Margin All Divisions

Net Profit is the profit you want to earn on top of the total cost. You can enter it as either a percentage or a monetary amount — the system automatically fills in the other.

If You Enter… The System Calculates… Result
Net Profit Amount (QAR) Net Profit % = (Amount ÷ Total Cost) × 100 Percentage is auto-filled
Net Profit % Net Profit Amount = Total Cost × (% ÷ 100) Amount is auto-filled

Net Profit After Discount

When a discount is applied, the effective net profit is:

Net Profit (after discount) Net Profit Amount = Gross Profit Amount − Discount Tolerance Amount
Net Profit % = Gross Profit % − Discount Tolerance %
🛈 In Simple Terms Gross Profit is your total markup across all items. Net Profit is what you actually keep after overheads and discounts are accounted for.

11. Discount Tolerance — How Much You Can Offer

Discount Tolerance is the maximum discount you are willing to give the client while still maintaining your target profit. It acts as a safety margin.

Field How It’s Calculated What It Means
Discount Tolerance Amount Total Bidding Price × (Discount % ÷ 100) The monetary value of the maximum discount.
Discount Tolerance % (Discount Amount ÷ Total Bidding Price) × 100 The discount as a percentage of the bidding price.
Discount Upto Total Bidding Price − Discount Tolerance Amount The lowest price you can accept after giving the full discount.
⚠ Important At the Cost Estimation level, discount tolerances from all Scope of Work lines are added together. Make sure each SoW’s discount is set correctly, as they all contribute to the overall discount tolerance.

12. Business Promotion

Business Promotion is an additional amount allocated for marketing, client relationship, and business development activities. Like overheads, it is calculated as a percentage of the Total Cost.

If You Enter… The System Calculates… Result
Business Promotion Amount Promotion % = (Amount ÷ Total Cost) × 100 Percentage is auto-filled
Business Promotion % Amount = Total Cost × (% ÷ 100) Amount is auto-filled

13. Total Bidding Price — The Final Quoted Price

The Total Bidding Price is the final amount you quote to your client. It includes the total cost, all overheads, your profit, and the discount tolerance buffer. The formula differs slightly between MEP and non-MEP divisions:

For Non-MEP Divisions Non-MEP

Total Bidding Price (Non-MEP) Total Bidding Price = Total Cost
  + Total Overheads (General + Engineering + Contingency)
  + Net Profit Amount
  + Discount Tolerance Amount

For MEP Division MEP Only

Total Bidding Price (MEP) Total Bidding Price = Total Cost
  + MEP Net Profit Amount
  + Discount Tolerance Amount
🛈 MEP Difference In the MEP Division, overheads are embedded within the Gross Profit calculation rather than added separately. The MEP Net Profit is:

MEP Net Profit = Gross Profit − (Overhead + Contingency + Engineering Overhead)

14. Unit Price — Price Per Unit of Work

For each Master Scope of Work (MSOW) line, the system calculates a Unit Price — the price per unit of that scope item:

Unit Price Unit Price = Total Bidding Price ÷ Quantity (of the MSOW)

This is the per-unit selling price that appears on your quotation.

15. MEP Division — Special Calculation Rules MEP Only

The MEP (Mechanical, Electrical & Plumbing) Division uses a different cost model for materials. MEP materials involve transfer charges between companies, which affects how cost and profit are calculated.

MEP Material Calculations

Field How It’s Calculated What It Means
Transfer Charges Valuation Rate × (Transfer % ÷ 100) The inter-company transfer charge per unit.
Transfer Amount Quantity × Transfer Charges Total transfer charges for the line.
Cost (with Transfer) Valuation Rate × (1 + Transfer % ÷ 100) Cost per unit including transfer charges.
Transfer Cost Amount Quantity × Cost (with Transfer) Total cost including transfer charges.
Selling Amount Quantity × Unit Price The selling price for the line.
Profit Amount Selling Amount − Transfer Cost Amount Profit after transfer costs.
Profit % (Profit Amount ÷ Selling Amount) × 100 Profit as a percentage of selling price.
🛈 Key MEP Differences
  • Material cost uses Transfer Cost Amount (not raw cost)
  • Material selling amount uses the Selling Amount directly
  • Total Overheads is set to 0 — overheads are embedded in the Gross Profit calculation
  • MEP Net Profit = Gross Profit − (Overhead + Contingency + Engineering Overhead)
